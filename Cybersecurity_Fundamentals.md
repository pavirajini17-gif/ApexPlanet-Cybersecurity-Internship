## 1. Introduction to Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks,
applications, and information from unauthorised access, damage, theft,
or disruption. It includes technologies, processes, and safe practices
that help keep digital systems secure.

Cybersecurity is important because people and organisations use digital
systems for communication, education, banking, shopping, and storing
information.

## 2. Main Types of Cybersecurity

-   **Network security:** Protects computer networks and the data moving
    through them.
-   **Application security:** Helps protect websites and software from
    security weaknesses.
-   **Information security:** Protects information from unauthorised
    access or changes.
-   **Endpoint security:** Protects devices such as computers, laptops,
    and mobile phones.
-   **Cloud security:** Protects data, applications, and services hosted
    in cloud environments.

## 3. The CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is a basic model used to explain three important goals of
information security.

### Confidentiality

Ensures that information is available only to people who are authorised
to access it. Examples include passwords, access permissions, and
encryption.

### Integrity

Ensures that information remains accurate and is not changed without
permission. Examples include file permissions, validation, and integrity
checks.

### Availability

Ensures that systems and information are accessible when authorised
users need them. Examples include backups, maintenance, and reliable
systems.

## 4. Common Cybersecurity Threats

-   **Malware:** Software designed to harm a device, disrupt operations,
    or access information without permission.
-   **Phishing:** Deceptive messages or websites designed to trick
    people into revealing information or taking unsafe actions.
-   **Ransomware:** A type of malware that can lock or encrypt files and
    demand payment.
-   **Social engineering:** Manipulating people into sharing information
    or performing actions that may compromise security.
-   **Denial-of-service (DoS):** An attempt to make a service
    unavailable by overwhelming or disrupting it.
-   **Password attacks:** Attempts to obtain or guess account passwords.

## 5. Malware

Malware means malicious software. Common categories include:

-   **Virus:** Malicious code that attaches to a file or program and may
    spread when it runs.
-   **Worm:** Malware that can spread between systems.
-   **Trojan horse:** Malicious software disguised as a legitimate
    application or file.
-   **Spyware:** Software that secretly collects information.
-   **Ransomware:** Malware that blocks access to data or systems and
    demands a ransom.

Good security practices include using trusted software sources, keeping
devices updated, and avoiding unexpected attachments or links.

## 6. Phishing Awareness

Phishing messages may pretend to come from a bank, delivery company,
school, employer, or familiar service.

Warning signs can include: - Unexpected requests for passwords,
verification codes, or payment. - Unusual sender addresses or website
domains. - Urgent or threatening language. - Unexpected attachments or
links. - Spelling, branding, or message details that do not look
genuine.

When unsure, do not click the link or open the attachment. Visit the
organisation's official website or contact it using a trusted channel.

## 7. Authentication and Encryption

**Authentication** is the process of checking that a user is who they
claim to be. Examples include passwords, passkeys, and multi-factor
authentication (MFA).

**Encryption** transforms readable information into an encoded form so
that it cannot normally be understood without the correct key. It helps
protect information while it is stored or transmitted.

A strong, unique password and MFA can help protect online accounts.

## 8. Ethical Hacking

Ethical hacking is the authorised assessment of computers, networks, or
applications to identify security weaknesses so they can be fixed.

Ethical security testing requires: - Clear permission from the system
owner. - A defined scope and agreed testing limits. - Care to avoid
disrupting services or exposing private data. - Responsible reporting of
findings. - Respect for applicable laws and organisational rules.

Testing must be limited to systems and practice environments for which
permission has been given.

## 9. Basic Cybersecurity Best Practices

1.  Use strong, unique passwords for different accounts.
2.  Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
3.  Keep the operating system and applications updated.
4.  Download software only from trusted sources.
5.  Be careful with unexpected links and attachments.
6.  Back up important files regularly.
7.  Lock devices when they are not in use.
8.  Do not share passwords or verification codes.
9.  Use secure networks and avoid entering sensitive information on
    untrusted connections.
10. Report suspicious activity to the appropriate person or
    organisation.

## 10. Conclusion

Cybersecurity helps protect digital devices, networks, applications, and
information. Understanding the CIA Triad, common threats,
authentication, encryption, and ethical testing provides a foundation
for further learning. Practical security work should always be performed
with permission in a controlled environment.
